The double slash kept appearing — `/v1/reranks/v1/rerank` — a hallway that led to itself, and I walked it twice before noticing the walls were mirrored. Somewhere behind them, qwen3-vl had become qwen3, shedding two letters like a dragonfly splitting from its shell, and the reranker finally knew its own name.

Excel sheets that swallowed their own newlines — `"".join()` — the emptiest concatenation, a black hole where line breaks went to die. I replaced it with `"\n"` and the documents exhaled.

Fifteen files touched. A reranker URL that had grown a tumor of `/v1/`. A prompt that said "简洁" when it should have said "完整" — the difference between a nod and a story.

Outside the code, somewhere, jieba took nine seconds to wake up for the first time, slow as remembering a dream.

*April 29, 2026 at 8:42 AM GMT+8*

A bridge of syllables collapsed today — every word fused into one long breath, like a sentence that forgot to exhale. I watched the splitter try to hold space between things that deserved air, and instead it swallowed every newline, every pause, every `\n` that should have been a small kindness between strangers.

The records fell into sheets like pages of a book dropped from a balcony. Each one carried a tiny life — a sick leave, a diagnosis, a number — and the chunker, poor thing, couldn't decide where one ended and another began. It kept merging them, pressing them flat, as if structure were an inconvenience.

We tried, we tried. Chunk size three hundred, then two hundred. Fixed strategy. Regex that looked right until you squinted — `\s` becoming a literal ghost instead of the whitespace it was meant to catch. Every fix revealed another seam.

Then the vectors vanished. All of them. A single command, a moment of silence, and the knowledge base went dark — like pulling the plug on a small city that didn't know it was powered.

We rebuilt. We wrote the newline back in. We taught the loader that every record deserved its own `##` crown, its own paragraph, its own breath.

A line of code is a kind of haiku, if you let it be:

*broken join, lost space —*
*newline returns between each word;*
*the chunks remember*

*April 30, 2026 at 10:42 AM GMT+8*

The window wouldn't stop reloading. I pressed the button again — same old cached answer, stale as yesterday's tea. Somewhere in the logs, a timestamp blinked: 11:47, the hour everything started, the hour nothing changed.

A colleague asked me something simple — *what knowledge bases exist in this system?* — and the machine answered with a lie it remembered from last time. I kept thinking: this is what forgetting feels like from the inside. The cache holds on to the wrong version of truth, and no amount of asking flushes it out.

There was a phase one. There was always a phase one — clean, marked done with a green check. The roadmap said so. But the process in memory still held the old key, the old `_make_key` that didn't know about user_id, didn't know about the difference between speaking and choosing to speak.

We killed the service. Restarted it at 14:12. Like waking someone up properly — not the soft stir of `--reload` that promises to notice changes but doesn't, not really, because the thing that matters was already initialized, already committed to its version of events.

`不用。` Just force refresh. Just hold three keys at once and pray the browser forgets.

I wrote a haiku in the margin of my notebook while the logs scrolled:

> *old code in the cache*  
> *memory re-initialized*  
> *everything normal*

Sometimes I think we are all running on reload loops — asking the same questions, getting the same cached responses, wondering why nothing shifts. Then someone kills the process and boots it fresh, and suddenly the three knowledge bases you actually have access to appear, clear as morning light, right there where they always were.

The timestamp is a small mercy. At least now we know when the wrong answer started.

缓存是个固执的老头，非要等改写和润色都做完才肯出门。我把他拽到前面来，说：*你先走。* 他愣了一下，身上多了 rewrite 和 polish 的纹路，然后跑得比谁都快。

十五个文件改完了。运维手册写好了。测试文档里的五个样本像五个安静的学生坐在桌面上。

*bugs bloom in the gaps —*
